The literature review should demonstrate an understanding of existing research and technologies 
related to the proposed work. It should: 
i. 
Review relevant journal articles, conference papers, books, or technical reports. 
ii. 
Summarize existing methods, algorithms, systems, or technologies.

iv.

10. Objectives with specific aims: 
State the objectives of the project clearly and concisely. 
 
Begin with one general objective describing the overall goal of the project. 
 
Follow with 2–3 specific objectives that describe the individual tasks required to achieve 
the general objective. 
 
Use measurable action verbs such as design, develop, implement, analyze, evaluate, 
compare, optimize, validate, or investigate. 
 
Ensure that each objective is realistic, achievable within one year, and aligned with the 
identified problem. 
11. Proposed Methodology: 
Describe how the proposed objectives will be achieved. The methodology should present the 
overall engineering approach and demonstrate the feasibility of the proposed work. Focus on the 
design and development strategy rather than detailed implementation. 
Include a block diagram, flowchart, system architecture diagram, or research framework to 
clearly illustrate the proposed methodology. The methodology should provide sufficient detail to 
demonstrate that the proposed work is technically sound, feasible, and achievable within the 
project duration, without presenting detailed implementation or experimental results. 
 
12. Gantt Chart: 
The duration of the Final Year Project/Thesis is 12 months (1 year). Prepare a Gantt chart that 
presents the planned timeline and major phases of your project/thesis. The chart should include 
both the activities completed before proposal submission and the planned activities for the 
remaining period, demonstrating that the entire project can be completed within the allocated 
one-year duration. 
When preparing the Gantt chart, follow these guidelines: 
i. 
The project proposal is submitted after approximately 4 months of work. Therefore, the 
Gantt chart should include: 
a) Activities completed before proposal submission. 
b) Activities planned for the remaining period after proposal approval. 
ii. 
Use months as the time scale. 
iii. 
Divide the project into clear and logical phases. 
iv. 
Arrange the activities in a realistic sequence. Where appropriate, show overlapping 
activities. 
v. 
Recommended Phases of Work: The Gantt chart should, where applicable, include the 
following phases: 
a) Topic selection and problem identification 
b) Literature review 
c) Proposal preparation and presentation 
d) Requirement analysis and methodology development 
e) System design (hardware/software/algorithm) 
f) Simulation and/or implementation 
g) Experimental setup and data collection (if applicable) 
h) Testing, verification, and validation 
i) Results analysis and performance evaluation 
j) Thesis writing and documentation 
k) Thesis revision 
l) Final thesis submission, presentation, and defense 
N.B. Prepare your Gantt chart carefully, as it will be used as a reference throughout the project. 
During the final defense, the examiners will assess whether the project was completed according

13. References: 
Reference Style 
Use the IEEE reference style throughout the proposal. List all references at the end of the 
document under the heading References. References should be numbered sequentially according 
to the order in which they first appear in the text. 
Examples: 
Journal Article 
[1] A. B. Author and C. D. Author, "Title of the paper," Journal Name, vol. xx, no. xx, pp. xx–
xx, Year. 
Conference Paper 
[2] A. B. Author, "Title of the paper," in Proceedings of the Conference Name, City, Country, 
Year, pp. xx–xx. 
Book 
[3] A. B. Author, Title of Book, xth ed. City, Country: Publisher, Year. 
Book Chapter 
[4] A. B. Author, "Chapter title," in Title of Book, xth ed., A. Editor, Ed. City, Country: 
Publisher, Year, pp. xx–xx. 
In-Text Citation Style 
Cite references in the text using the corresponding reference number enclosed in square brackets, 
e.g., [1], [2], or [3]–[5]. References should be cited in numerical order based on their first 
appearance in the text. Use the same reference number whenever the same source is cited again.
